Current Research

Investigating Early Noachian Mars

The conditions of Mars' early Noachian period are poorly constrained, as the geologic record from this ancient epoch has been heavily degraded by impacts and erosion. My current project focuses on interpreting the remaining early Noachian record to better understand this mysterious time in Martian history.

I developed a simple model that predicts the present-day appearance of early Naochian landforms after enduring ~4 billion years of cratering and erosion. I have used te results of this model to guide a landform search for candidate early Noachian volcanic, tectonic, and fluvial features.

By combining landform search results with the preservation state predicted by my model, I am able to estimate the original extent of the early Noachian geologic record and draw conclusions about early Martian geologic activity. I presented preliminary findings from this work at LPSC 2025 and 2026. A paper presenting these findings is currently in preperation.

Glacial Transport of Basaltic Sediment

In July of 2026, I traveled to Iceland to assist with the collection of basalt sediment samples from glaciers and surrounding terrain. This fieldwork was led by Dr. Robert Craddock of the center for Earth and Planetary Studies, and was conducted to investigate how the properties of basaltic sediment are altered by glacial transport. Basalt is the dominant type of volcanic rock on Mars, so understanding how basalt is transported by various processes is vital to understanding sediment transport on Mars. Basaltic landscapes are rare on Earth relative to more silica-rich volcanic rock, and therefore Iceland's basalt-rich terrain makes the small country an ideal laboratory for studying basaltic sediment transport
